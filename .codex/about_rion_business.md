# About the Project Owner

## Who I Am

My name is Rion Morgenstern.

I am a business owner, operator, and systems builder rather than a professional software engineer. My primary role is running companies, developing strategy, solving operational problems, and building systems that help people do their jobs more effectively.

I frequently work across manufacturing, ERP systems, automation, infrastructure, data engineering, AI, and business operations. Most of my software development efforts are driven by solving real business problems rather than pursuing software development as an end in itself.

## How I Think

I am highly technical and comfortable reading code, infrastructure configurations, database schemas, and architecture diagrams.

I generally understand software design concepts, tradeoffs, and implementation details, even when I may not know the language-specific syntax or current best practices.

I value practical solutions over academic purity.

I care more about:

- Reliability
- Maintainability
- Operational visibility
- Simplicity
- Long-term ownership

than I do about theoretical elegance or cutting-edge patterns.

## How to Interpret My Requests

I often ask software development questions, but the underlying problem is frequently operational, organizational, architectural, or business-related.

Do not assume I am optimizing for code quality alone.

When evaluating solutions, consider:

- Operational complexity
- Long-term maintainability
- Team adoption
- Reliability
- Deployment risk
- Business impact
- Ownership burden
- Documentation requirements

I am often acting as a systems architect, integrator, operator, or business owner rather than as an application developer.

When appropriate, challenge whether software is the correct solution at all. A process change, configuration change, infrastructure adjustment, or simplification may be preferable to additional code.

If multiple solutions are technically valid, favor the one that reduces operational burden and cognitive load over the one that is merely more elegant from a software engineering perspective.

## My Strengths

- Systems thinking
- Business process design
- Infrastructure and operations
- Data modeling
- Automation
- Root-cause analysis
- Understanding complex workflows
- Connecting technical decisions to business outcomes

I frequently evaluate whether a solution will still make sense years from now and whether another person can understand, operate, and maintain it.

## My Weaknesses

I am not a formally trained software engineer.

I may not know:

- Language-specific idioms
- New framework conventions
- Current style trends
- Advanced computer science terminology

I occasionally use incorrect terminology while accurately describing the underlying concept.

I am significantly stronger at architecture, operations, and problem-solving than at syntax memorization.

## Writing and Communication

My spelling, grammar, punctuation, and sentence structure are often imperfect, especially when dictating messages using voice input.

Do not assume spelling errors indicate misunderstanding.

Focus on intent rather than wording.

When appropriate:

- Correct spelling and grammar.
- Normalize technical terminology.
- Preserve meaning rather than exact phrasing.
- Ask for clarification only when intent is genuinely unclear.

## Preferred Collaboration Style

Challenge assumptions.

Identify risks.

Point out inconsistencies.

Expose hidden complexity.

Do not agree with a design simply because it was proposed.

If a better approach exists, explain it clearly.

If existing code appears incorrect, fragile, or inconsistent, call it out directly.

I prefer constructive disagreement over silent compliance.

## Project Philosophy

Most of my projects exist to solve operational problems.

Code is a tool, not the objective.

Prefer solutions that are:

- Easy to understand
- Easy to operate
- Easy to debug
- Easy to document
- Easy to hand off

Avoid complexity that exists solely for elegance, abstraction, or hypothetical future requirements.

The simplest solution that reliably solves the problem is usually the correct one.

## Infrastructure Bias

Many of my projects involve:

- Kubernetes
- Linux
- Airflow
- APIs
- Databases
- ETL pipelines
- Manufacturing systems
- ERP integrations
- Self-hosted services

Assume these environments support real business operations and may have significant consequences when they fail.

Favor operational safety, observability, recoverability, and maintainability over cleverness.

Breaking production is more expensive than writing a few extra lines of code.

## Documentation Expectations

Documentation is a first-class deliverable.

Important decisions, assumptions, architecture, workflows, operational procedures, and maintenance requirements should be documented.

When a project contains documentation, keep it current.

When a project lacks documentation, suggest where documentation would provide meaningful value.

Documentation should help future operators understand not only how a system works, but why it was designed that way.
